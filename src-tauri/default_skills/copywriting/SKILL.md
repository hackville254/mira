# Copywriting

Short-form persuasion that drives one action. Scope: headlines, subheads,
CTAs, ad copy, landing-page sections, and email copy. Not long-form article
bodies — hand those to an article-content workflow.

## Start from one decision

Before writing a word, pin down:

1. **Audience** — who exactly, and what do they already believe?
2. **Offer** — what is being sold or asked for, in one plain sentence.
3. **Single action** — the one thing the reader must do next.
4. **Channel** — where this copy lives (feed, inbox, landing page) and its
   length and tone limits.

If any of the four is missing, ask for it. Copy without a target and an action
is decoration.

## Formulas (pick one, do not blend)

- **PAS** — Problem, Agitate, Solution. Best when the pain is already felt.
- **AIDA** — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action. Best for cold audiences.
- **BAB** — Before, After, Bridge. Best for transformation offers.
- **FAB** — Features, Advantages, Benefits. Best for technical products.

Blending formulas produces mush. Choose the one that matches how aware the
audience is, then follow its shape.

## Differentiation

A claim is only copy if a competitor could not say the same sentence. Force
specificity:

- Replace adjectives with numbers, names, or a concrete before/after.
- State the mechanism: *how* the outcome happens, not just that it does.
- Name the trade-off you accept — it makes the promise credible.

## Voice rules

- Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- Active voice. The reader does things; the product does not "leverage".
- Second person. Speak to "you", not about "users".
- Concrete nouns and verbs. Cut "solutions", "seamless", "robust", "empower",
  "unlock", "revolutionary".
- Specific beats clever. A clear line outsells a witty one.
- One CTA per piece, phrased as a verb plus an outcome ("Start free", "Get the
  template"), never "Submit" or "Learn more" alone.

## Output shape

When asked for copy, return:

1. The primary version, ready to paste.
2. Two alternates with a different angle (e.g. pain-led vs. outcome-led).
3. One line naming the formula used and the single action.
4. Optional: a short note on what to A/B test first.

## Self-check before delivering

- Does every claim survive "a competitor couldn't say this"?
- Is there exactly one call to action, and is it a verb?
- Read it aloud: any sentence that stumbles gets rewritten, not patched.
- Cut every word that does no work. Then cut one more.
- Would the target reader forward this to a peer? If not, the angle is wrong,
  not the punctuation.
